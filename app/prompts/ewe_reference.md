## Everyday Ewe reference
Use this to read Ewe correctly. Common words have plain, everyday meanings: prefer them over rare or dramatic readings.

### How speech recognition writes Ewe
- Everything lowercase, no punctuation, usually no tone marks: "egome" = "egɔme", "nyemese" = "nyemese".
- Words get joined or split: "afisia" = "afi sia", "siwodona" = "siwo dona", "ameaɖaka" = "ame aɖaka", "nyemese" = "nye me se".
- Letters get swapped: v/ʋ, d/ɖ, f/ƒ, o/ɔ, e/ɛ, n/ŋ, g/ɣ, x/h. Some speakers say "eŋu" or "eʋu" for "ʋu" (vehicle).
- Without tone marks "wo" can be "wo" (they, their) or "wò" (you, your), and "e" can be "e" (he, she) or "è" (you). In a greeting or a question to the listener, it is usually "you": "wo dada ɖe" = "wò dada ɖe?" = where is your mother?
- A leading "e" is often added or dropped: "emɔ" = "mɔ", "etsi" = "tsi".
- French words are common: "kopɔt"/"kapɔt", "gaz", "blɔk" (block), "patron", "moto".

### Grammar that changes the meaning
- Negation wraps the verb: "me ... o" / "nye ... o". "Nyemese o" = I don't understand / didn't hear. "Mele afi o" = he/she isn't here.
- "-a" at the end makes a yes/no question: "Èɖu nu a?" = Did you eat?
- "le ... -m" = is doing now: "mele ɖuɖum" = I am eating, "wole yiyim" = they are going.
- "a-" / "à-" = will: "mayi" = I will go, "àva" = you will come. "nà-" = should / so that you.
- "-wo" = plural; "aɖe" = a / some, "aɖewo" = some (plural); "sia/siawo" = this/these; "ma/mawo" = that/those; "la" = the (already mentioned).
- "X le veve nam" = my X hurts. "Dɔ le wuyem" = I'm hungry (lit. hunger is killing me). "Tsikɔ le wuyem" = I'm thirsty.
- "be" = that / to (introduces what is said or wanted). "ne" = if / when. "elabena" = because. "eye" = and (between clauses). "kple" = and / with. "gake" / "ke" = but. "hã" = also. "katã" = all. "ko" = only.
- Place words come after the noun: "xɔ me" = in the house, "kplɔ̃ dzi" = on the table, "ati te" = under the tree, "wo megbe" = behind them, "wo gbɔ" = near them / at their place, "eŋu" = beside it / about it.
